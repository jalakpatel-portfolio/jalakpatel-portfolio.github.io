Context

Short-form had to serve the long form

Shorts and Reels were not treated as a separate brand or an unrelated stream of content. They drew from the same shoots and subject matter, allowing one production effort to serve more than one format. When a long video was not live yet, or fresh footage was delayed, short-form content and community posts offered another way to stay connected with the audience.

Recovery and creative problem-solving

Build a story around the footage that survived

02 / 04

The constraint

Recipe footage was corrupted, and a reshoot was not possible. Rather than discard the usable material, I shaped a story around the clips that remained.

For the first recovery attempts, I wrote a funny narrative that could work with the surviving footage, turning a production setback into a different kind of short-form story. After exploring that treatment more than once, I took a different creative direction for a third Reel: a horror-style treatment in which I also appeared on camera. The experiment received positive feedback for trying a new direction, although it was not the strongest-performing version.

Takeaway

One shoot,
two surfaces

Plan short-form with the long form, not after the fact.

Short-form gave longer content another route to the audience, while community posts helped fill the gaps between uploads. Recovery work also required adapting the story to the footage available rather than relying on a reshoot.

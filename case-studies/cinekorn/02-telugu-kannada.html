Case 02 · Telugu and Kannada

Telugu and Kannada channel systems

Regional language channels with their own catalogue logic so distribution matched language demand, not a single pan-India feed.

Context

Language is a distribution decision

Telugu and Kannada audiences needed dedicated surfaces. A single Hindi or pan-India feed could not carry regional title demand with the same clarity of selection, titles, and cadence.

Building language channels meant treating each as its own system: catalogue choices, upload rhythm, and how performance is read against that language market.

Strategy

Separate systems, shared discipline

Run language channels with consistent publishing rules while keeping title selection matched to each audience. The goal was ownership of the surface - not occasional regional uploads on a general channel.

  • Define what belongs on Telugu vs Kannada vs other properties.
  • Keep measurement comparable across channels without mixing lifetime catalogue into tenure claims.
  • Use analytics surfaces to steer what gets prioritised next.

Evidence

Channel analytics

Public analytics surfaces used while operating the regional systems. They support the operating view, not a single portfolio-wide growth percentage.

Results

What this case claims

This case shows the system design and the evidence surfaces used to run it. It does not publish an unverified combined growth rate for both languages. Tenure performance is kept separate from lifetime catalogue wherever the archive supports that distinction.

Takeaway

Regional channels need ownership

Language surfaces work when catalogue selection and cadence are intentional - not when they are leftovers from a central feed. The same discipline that protects measurement also protects the audience experience.

A Little Bit
of Home

A 30-second ad-film concept for a fictional Indian spice-blend brand. The story uses one familiar sensory detail, the sound and aroma of tadka, to turn a routine city evening into a moment of connection.

Format30-second brand film
AudienceYoung adults living away from family
Role exploredStrategy, concept, script and pre-production plan

01 · The brief

Make a familiar product feel personal

Communication task

Give a fictional everyday spice blend a memorable emotional territory without relying on a list of ingredients or generic claims about taste.

Audience tension

For someone living in a city away from home, cooking can feel like one more task after work. Small familiar rituals can make an unfamiliar place feel warmer.

The brand, product and brief are fictional for this portfolio exercise. This page documents the proposed creative, not a commissioned campaign or a completed shoot.

02 · Audience insight

Home can arrive before the first bite

A familiar cooking sound or aroma can bring back a place and a person before the meal is even ready.

Strategic choice: Show the sensory trigger first, then reveal the emotional shift. The product earns its place in the story by starting the ritual, not by interrupting it with a hard sell.

03 · The concept

The first tadka changes the room

A young professional returns to a quiet apartment after a long day. As spices hit warm oil, the sound momentarily carries us into a remembered family kitchen. The memory is brief and impressionistic; back in the present, the protagonist invites a neighbour in to share dinner. The closing line connects the product to a feeling, not an unprovable product claim.

Visual treatment

Begin with cool, still city frames and practical light. At the cooking moment, move into warm close-ups, shallow focus and tactile details: oil, spices, steam, a hand stirring. Return to the present with a warmer frame and a little more movement.

Sound treatment

Let the sizzle lead the transition. A soft remembered kitchen ambience sits underneath for a moment, then blends into the apartment's natural sound. Keep music restrained so the cooking sound remains the recognisable cue.

04 · Script and timing

30-second film script

TimePictureSound / copy
0–4 secWide shot: a small city apartment. A tired young professional drops keys beside an unopened takeaway menu.Room tone, traffic outside. No dialogue.
4–8 secClose-up: oil warms in a pan. The spice blend is added; particles hit the oil.A clear, close sizzle. Music begins very softly.
8–14 secMatch cut into a remembered family kitchen: hands preparing dinner, steam on a window, a familiar serving bowl. No faces required.The sizzle carries through. Brief kitchen ambience; a warm memory, not a literal flashback scene.
14–21 secBack in the apartment. The protagonist smiles, plates the food and sends a message: “Made extra. Dinner?”Natural kitchen sounds. Music opens slightly.
21–26 secA neighbour arrives. Two plates land on the table; the room feels less quiet.Door chime, plates, a small laugh. No forced dialogue.
26–30 secPack shot: fictional spice-blend pouch beside the pan and finished dish. Clean, warm frame.Super: “A little bit of home.” End card: fictional brand mark and product name.

The script avoids a medical, nutritional or superiority claim. Product packaging and brand identity would be developed as part of the production design.

05 · Shot list

Priority coverage for a compact shoot

Performance and story

  1. Apartment establishing shot and keys close-up.
  2. Medium shot of the protagonist entering and decompressing.
  3. Hands adding spice blend to the pan.
  4. Reaction close-up when the aroma registers.
  5. Message insert, door arrival and shared table two-shot.

Product and sensory details

  1. Hero macro of spice blend falling into oil.
  2. Steam and stirring details, with clean backgrounds.
  3. Memory inserts: hands, bowl, window light and serving motion.
  4. Food plating and final table detail.
  5. Pack shot with label facing camera and safe text space.

07 · Edit and delivery plan

Make the feeling land before the logo

  • First assembly: protect the emotional arc and keep the memory section short enough to feel like a sensory association.
  • Sound pass: make the sizzle a clear transition cue; avoid over-scoring the scene.
  • Colour pass: separate the cooler present from the warmer memory, then bring the final present-day scene into a warmer balance.
  • Brand pass: verify pack legibility, end-card duration, text contrast and spelling.
  • Delivery: master in 16:9, then reframe intentionally for 9:16 and 1:1 rather than cropping the final pack shot blindly.

Scope and honesty note

What this case demonstrates

This independent exercise demonstrates a proposed brief interpretation, audience insight, concept development, scriptwriting and a pre-production approach. No shoot, finished film, client approval, media spend or performance result is being claimed.

SWAD COOKING INSTITUTE

Retention and conversion systems for food media. Institute and owner channels operated as connected growth systems.

Role: Social Media Manager & Content Lead · Period: Nov 2024 to Oct 2025

About the company & role

B2C courses — content that has to serve more than one audience

Swad Cooking Institute sells cooking and baking education: offline hobby and diploma batches in Surat, online programs, workshops, and longer career-oriented courses. Unlike Ajmera and Jalan (B2B textiles aimed at Tier 2/3 retail buyers), Swad’s product is training — and the same brand had to speak to different people on different platforms without collapsing into one generic “food page.”

That multi-audience problem was the core strategic challenge: one institute, one product family, several real customer types, each with a different reason to watch, enrol, or return.

How the audiences actually split

YouTube lives vs uploaded videos. Live audiences skewed toward viewers around 35+ — often home cooks who treat daily lives like a serial episode: familiar faces, routine, and real-time presence. That is why lives drove strong concurrent views and chat engagement. Uploaded recipe videos pulled a different behaviour: viewers who mainly want the recipe or technique, with less emotional attachment to a specific chef. Same channel, two jobs.

Revenue vs career leads. YouTube primarily supported ad revenue and lighter online workshop interest. It was not the main engine for heavy career products. The one-year diploma and similar high-intent courses leaned more on word of mouth, restaurant relationships and internship pathways Swad already had, plus Instagram and Facebook — not on treating every YouTube viewer as a diploma prospect.

Offline hobby classes in Surat. Local students often start with a short hobby batch nearby, then convert later into diploma or food-business tracks. Offline workshops and classroom cohorts are a different funnel from national YouTube reach: proximity, trial, then deeper commitment.

Instagram as mix and acquisition. Instagram carried a wider age mix. It worked best as promotion and as a way to bring in a younger, cooking-curious segment — not as a copy of the YouTube live serial, and not as the full diploma sales floor.

Strategy meant matching format and platform to the audience that actually converts there — instead of forcing one message across every surface.

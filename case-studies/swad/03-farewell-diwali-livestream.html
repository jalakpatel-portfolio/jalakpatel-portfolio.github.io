Case 03 · Audience tribute · Swad Cooking Institute

The Diwali multi-chef livestream

Six chefs came together for a festive grazing-table menu, with more than 15 recipes across one continuous 5h 34m livestream. It was also the last major original production I built at Swad, though the stream itself was presented simply as a Diwali celebration.

5h 34m continuous live 15+ recipes 6 chefs ~70K archived views

Context

A festive table with the full teaching team

The idea was to bring the teaching bench together on one Diwali stream. The chefs contributed dish ideas, and I shaped them into one Indian grazing-table and party-menu format that could work as a continuous live session.

We wanted to give viewers useful festive food ideas, showcase the faculty and do something generous for the audience. This was not planned as a dashboard or growth project. I spent about a week planning the menu and format, with the kitchen preparation and mise en place locked before the day because of the number of dishes, garnishes and presentation details.

For me, it was also a quiet personal close. It was my last major original production at Swad, but I did not announce that on stream. The team and viewers experienced it as a Diwali celebration.

Execution

Turning separate ideas into one live format

I shaped the menu and running order before the event, then coordinated the flow from behind the camera once the stream began.

From separate chef ideas to one continuous live

The production moved through five connected stages. I coordinated the editorial plan and live flow with the chefs and wider team.

01 · PLANMenu developmentRecipe pool, primary and backup dishes, no repeats, chef strengths and festive fit.
02 · STRUCTURERun-of-showCourse order, balanced screen time, chef timers and handoff buffers.
03 · PREPAREPre-live readinessKitchen mise en place, social stories, broadcast path, thumbnail and title.
04 · DIRECTLive coordinationChef order, transitions and team support across the stream setup.
05 · CONNECTAudience and recoveryLive-chat questions, kitchen communication and an unexpected host change.

Planning the menu and running order

I gathered a wider pool of dish ideas, then planned a primary recipe and a backup with each chef. I checked for repeated dishes, Diwali and YouTube fit, and negotiated changes when ideas clashed.

I built the run-of-show around variety and attention across a long session. I balanced screen time, placed starters and smaller plates before desserts, and set a timer for each chef. On the day, I directed the order of appearances and handoffs. We planned to start in the early afternoon, and the live ran about 35 minutes longer than planned.

Directing the live and connecting the audience

I prepared the pre-live promotion across the broadcast path and social stories, and worked on the thumbnail and title. Behind the camera, I stayed as the bridge between the kitchen and live chat, answering common questions during mixing, cooking and waiting time and passing relevant ones to the chef on screen.

The live operations were a team effort. I supported the setup across the phone stream, MacBook, power, cooling and microphone rather than treating the technical side as a solo job.

One segment needed a change in the moment. It was Chef Siddhant's first appearance on Swad's YouTube channel, and we had planned it with Chef Rakhi to help him settle in. Rakhi had to leave midway through the recipe. I supported Siddhant by explaining the method and handling viewer questions using my bakery knowledge, so the segment could continue.

Takeaway

A long live needs more than a running order

A multi-chef stream depends on menu decisions, timing, handoffs and a steady connection between the people cooking and the people watching. This one also showed me how a segment can keep moving when a planned on-screen pairing changes unexpectedly.

Not every meaningful piece of work is a growth story. I was glad to leave the team and audience with a full festive production, without making my personal goodbye part of the broadcast.

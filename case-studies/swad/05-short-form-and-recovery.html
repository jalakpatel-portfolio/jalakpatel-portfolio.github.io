Short-form engine and recovery

A short-form system tied to long videos, plus recovery work when footage was lost.

Context

Short-form had to serve the long form

Shorts and Reels were not treated as a separate brand. They pulled from the same shoots and topics so the same work fed both formats. When a long video was not live yet, or when new footage was delayed, the short-form surface and community posts kept the audience present.

Between uploads

Community posts

When a video was not live or a new upload was not out yet, I used YouTube community posts to keep the audience engaged. The aim was to bring people back, invite replies and encourage shares.

Recovery and creative problem-solving

Build a story around the footage that survived

When recipe footage was corrupted and a reshoot was not possible, I worked with the usable clips and wrote a funny story around them instead of discarding the material. After exploring that approach more than once, I tried a different genre for a third Reel: a horror treatment in which I also appeared. The experiment received positive feedback for trying a new direction, though it was not the strongest-performing version.

Takeaway

One shoot, two surfaces

Short-form works best when it is planned with the long form, not after the fact. Community posts filled the gaps between uploads so the audience never went quiet.

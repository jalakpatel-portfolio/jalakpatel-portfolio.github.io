Case 04 · Owner channel strategy · Swad Cooking Institute

Building a publishing system for a separate owner channel

Chef Sneha Thakkar Recipes launched as its own recorded channel, not a second feed of the institute. At handover it had about 1,500 subscribers and three videos already published by the team. From November 2024 to October 2025 I owned content strategy, metadata and publishing direction, then on-set direction and final edit approval once the system was stable.

Context

A channel with a different job

Swad Institute ran lives and recipe uploads through the week, with no Sunday slot. Sneha Thakkar Recipes was built as a recorded catalogue with a Friday publish day. The owner channel stayed recipe-led. Course promotion stayed on the institute side.

Sneha Thakkar Recipes

A recorded recipe catalogue

Friday publishing, focused on recipes. No course promotion on this channel.

Friday-only publishing was the company’s idea. I stopped Friday lives on the institute channel so the owner channel had a clearer weekly space and the recipe calendar was less stretched.

The first full metadata pass under my system was the white dhokla upload, the same video featured below as the first system upload. In the first 24 hours it reached about 30,000 views. That response set the pattern for how we treated titles, descriptions and packaging after that.

Strategy

Publishing rules for an owner catalogue

Four decisions shaped how we planned, packaged and produced the channel’s recorded catalogue.

01

Metadata as a system

I rebuilt titles, descriptions, tags and Hindi ingredient language, then improved them video by video. Early descriptions leaned on long keyword titles and bilingual ingredient lists. Later uploads used a clearer structure: a short about line, ingredients and method in parts, chef tips, a cleaner keyword block and hashtags.

02

Calendar in advance

At first we chose a recipe, shot and uploaded in the same week. That was too tight. We moved to a one-month bank, then to about two months of scheduled content so shoots and other work did not collide.

03

A simple monthly mix

Across a month we aimed for one bakery or sweet, one authentic or basic recipe, one fried or snack item, and one unique piece or a recipe linked to an older upload. Season, festival and trend guided the choices.

04

Direction after the system held

Publishing and metadata came first. After about two to three months, when the Friday rhythm was stable, direction on set became part of every shoot. I focused on speech, angles, extra coverage, food presentation and final packaging.

Shorts were not a fixed count per long video. Output depended on the recipe and what the shoot could support. Dual horizontal and vertical capture was reserved for heavier days.

On-set direction and final approval

  • Ran the steady front camera
  • Guided speech, pace and delivery
  • Suggested angles and additional coverage
  • Guided plating and food detail shots
  • Reviewed the edit and took the final call, usually after a second opinion from the team

How the production team worked

The cinematographer handled gimbal close-ups, movement and extra footage. The editor cut the videos. I reviewed and improved the edit, then made the final decision after the direction phase began.

When a shoot needed more coverage

On larger shoot days we used two cinematographers when needed: one path for the long video and one for vertical reels. The weekly meal-planning shoot ran from morning into the evening. The script grew while we shot, and we split the final piece into two parts so the runtime stayed usable. Keeping the team and the chef moving through that day was part of the job.

Takeaway

What this case shows

Case 02 and Case 06 describe institute systems. This case is the owner channel only.

It shows a recorded Friday catalogue with no course promotion on that surface, a metadata and calendar system that moved from same-week scramble to advance planning, a simple monthly mix of recipe types, and direction on every shoot once publishing was stable.

Case 04 · Reading the tracker

Reading about fifty accounts without overclaiming

The tracker covered YouTube, Facebook, Instagram and other platforms. This case explains the comparison rules and the results that remain unverified.

Context

A tracker is only useful if the dates match

I maintained a tracker across YouTube, Facebook, Instagram and other platforms. To make a comparison useful, the account list and date window need to match. New properties should not be mixed into the starting set, and the metric definition needs to stay consistent across both dates.

Scale

Keep the comparison fair

The available archive does not currently verify the combined reach figures well enough to publish a portfolio-wide percentage. I have left that headline number out rather than present an unresolved result as fact.

01
Match the account list

Compare only properties present at both measurement points.

02
Match the dates

Use the same start and end dates for every account in the comparison.

03
Keep metric definitions consistent

Do not combine different platform metrics as if they were interchangeable.

After I left

What the public numbers show later

Public channel numbers after my tenure are shown where available so the case does not stop at the day I left. Internal files such as the tracker itself are not published; the numbers are shown, the files are not.

What this showed

Show the method before the headline

A comparison is only persuasive when the account list, dates, and metric definitions can be reproduced. Until the underlying tracker values are verified, the method is the evidence this case can responsibly show.
